Welcome

This landing page will contain an image and a short text about the reasoning behind the book and what it is trying to achieve.

This books takes an developmental approach to visualization motivating the introduction of each new principle or chart element with the shortcoming of the previous. This is because I have noticed that students of dat avisualization don’t neceessarily understand when to use one type of chart instead of another. Now they might still remember it becauase they have memorized some rule like “never use bar chart for a mean” and then happily use a single point for the mean that commit similar fouls without inspecting the underlying distribution of data. Understanding instead of remembering is a way to empower students to become their own agents and make predictions about what is good and what is bad outside the content of this book. I hope to instill them with a critical thinking about any chart they might see and not just remember that a certain chart time is good and another one is bad. In my experience,that also makes things more fun.

To make the learning interactive, i also include code in many different libraries. This focuses on grammars of graphics because I think they do empower student in a similar way as descrbed above; ie the predicatable grammar encourages students to understand what could work based on understasnding the structure and rules of the grammar rather than being focused on what does work from remembering a full api catalogue of chart templtaes and names.

Just like development does not recapitulate the stags of evolution, there progression herein is not a reflection of the history of data visualization although this author think it could have been.

This books also hopes to introduce students to some interesting data sets along the way, although this will likely be a work in progress over a longer time as I come across more suitable ones.

This books take an exercise first approach and interleaves exercises throughout the chapters. These are largely focused on principles rather than coding, since memorization of library APIs seems to be an increasingly replaceable skill (it is still important to understand the principles of the grammar of graphics, but the exact code for each existing implementation is less interesting although still useful). I believe learning and exercises are tighyl coupled so therefore exercises are spread throughout the chapters rather than all coming at the end; this doesn’t mean there is not value in doing the exercises after reading as repetition. experiential learning is best, in exercises student can experience the problem,not just “read about it”
